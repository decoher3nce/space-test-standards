- fft_solar_array_deployment: For solar arrays the deployment test is mandatory before and after the environmental tests, with a manual deployment beforehand
- solar_see_20_08: For solar arrays and solar panels this test is governed by ECSS-E-ST-20-08
- dedicated_model: Performed on a dedicated model
- life_solar_panel_20_08: Solar panel life testing is covered by ECSS-E-ST-20-08
- burn_in_parallel: Run in parallel with the other functional and environmental tests
- cog_moi_by_calculation: With customer agreement, centre of gravity and moments of inertia may be calculated instead of measured
- one_of_three_strength_tests: One of static load, spin or sine burst is performed when strength is not already covered by the sinusoidal vibration test
- random_or_acoustic_by_size: For antennas, optical equipment and mechanisms, random vibration, acoustic or both are chosen according to the type, size and location of the equipment
- solar_array_random_added_to_acoustic: For solar arrays fixed directly to the element side wall without an offset bracket, random vibration should be added to the acoustic test
- shock_not_needed_if_insensitive: The shock test may be omitted when the equipment is shown to tolerate levels above the shock environment
- solar_array_shock_at_component_level: Solar array shock qualification is done at component level and confirmed during the deployment test
- microvib_emission_if_source: Performed when the customer requires it because the equipment may generate micro-vibrations that degrade the mission
- microvib_susceptibility_if_sensitive: Performed when the equipment's function or performance may be degraded by micro-vibrations
- electronic_sealed_or_pressurized_only: For electronic, electrical and RF equipment these structural-integrity tests are mandatory only when the equipment is sealed or pressurised
- battery_pressure_tests_at_cell_level: For batteries, proof pressure, pressure cycling and burst are performed at cell (component) level
- burst_dedicated_model_or_last: Performed on a dedicated model or as the final test of the QM programme
- solar_panel_tv_plus_dvt_coupon: For solar panels thermal vacuum is complemented by thermal tests on the design verification test coupon per ECSS-E-ST-20-08
- mission_pressure_applicability: Mission-pressure cycling without vacuum cycling applies only to equipment operating in a non-vacuum environment for its whole life; consider depressurisation failure when deciding
- mission_pressure_combination: Mission-pressure cycling and vacuum cycling may be combined in sequence
- no_room_pressure_cycling: Room-pressure temperature cycling (US-style thermal cycling, ECSS-E-ST-10-03A) is not part of this standard
- emc_bonding_only_without_electronics: For equipment without electronics the EMC test reduces to a bonding test
- magnetic_if_mission_needs: Performed when justified by mission needs, in line with the EMC control plan
- esd_solar_20_08: For solar arrays and panels ESD testing is covered by ECSS-E-ST-20-08
- corona_applicability: Applicability conditions are given in clause 5.5.5.6 (exposure to the critical low-pressure range)
- audible_noise_crewed_only: Required only for equipment of crewed space segment elements

### Rules

#### Functional and performance tests bracket the equipment test programme and each environmental block

`ecss-e-st-10-03c-rev1.rule.equipment_functional_tests_around_environments` · functional_test · §5.1, p. 42, pdf 42 · confidence: verified · [standards/ecss-e-st-10-03c-rev1/rules/functional-test-sequence.yaml](standards/ecss-e-st-10-03c-rev1/rules/functional-test-sequence.yaml)

Equipment follows the Figure 5-1 sequence ("test like you fly": environments in flight order, defects found as early as possible), filtered by the applicability tables (5.1c). Performance and full functional tests are run at the start and end of the programme under clean-room conditions and should agree within test tolerances (5.1e); a reduced functional test is run before and after each environmental block and around transportation (5.1f); PT, FFT or RFT as relevant are run during thermal tests and whenever the equipment is operational under an imposed environment (5.1g). Equipment operated during ascent or descent is powered and monitored in that mode during the environmental tests (5.1h). Unusual behaviour is trended (5.1d); adjustable protections are tested (5.1j). Elements: visual inspections before and after each test (6.1g); the sequence is agreed with the customer according to how performance is verified (6.1d).

#### Development tests are completed before qualification and never run on QM or FM hardware

`ecss-e-st-10-03c-rev1.rule.development_tests_before_qualification` · model_philosophy · §4.2, p. 24, pdf 24 · confidence: verified · [standards/ecss-e-st-10-03c-rev1/rules/model-philosophy.yaml](standards/ecss-e-st-10-03c-rev1/rules/model-philosophy.yaml)

Development testing of a product is finished before its formal qualification testing starts (4.2a); development tests may run over operating conditions beyond the design range. They are not performed on qualification or flight models or parts of them (4.2b). Configuration, results and other pertinent data are kept on record for later failure investigations (4.2c).

#### Qualification on dedicated models; destructive and life-consuming tests kept off flight hardware

`ecss-e-st-10-03c-rev1.rule.qualification_on_dedicated_models` · model_philosophy · §4.5.2, p. 36, pdf 36 · confidence: verified · [standards/ecss-e-st-10-03c-rev1/rules/model-philosophy.yaml](standards/ecss-e-st-10-03c-rev1/rules/model-philosophy.yaml)

Qualification testing is done on dedicated qualification models unless the protoflight approach is used (4.5.2b). Destructive tests such as burst use a representative model other than the QM, or are run last in the qualification programme (4.5.2e); with a PFM they always use a separate representative model (4.5.4b). For equipment under a protoflight approach, life, burst pressure and ESD are qualification tests performed on a dedicated model and never on the PFM (5.4b). Acceptance testing is performed on every flight product except the one used as protoflight (4.5.3b), after the qualification programme is complete (4.5.3c).

#### Protoflight approach uses qualification levels with acceptance durations; tabulated durations are minima

`ecss-e-st-10-03c-rev1.rule.protoflight_levels_and_durations` · model_philosophy · §4.5.4, p. 38, pdf 38 · confidence: verified · [standards/ecss-e-st-10-03c-rev1/rules/model-philosophy.yaml](standards/ecss-e-st-10-03c-rev1/rules/model-philosophy.yaml)

Protoflight testing on the first flight model combines the qualification objective (performance with qualification margins) and the acceptance objective (freedom from workmanship and material defects). The general approach selects qualification test levels and acceptance test durations; the durations in Tables 5-2, 5-4, 5-6, 6-2, 6-4 and 6-6 are minimum values. The protoflight approach may be applied at any level of decomposition, and an element PFM programme can include tests on dedicated models later refurbished into the PFM (for example a structural model for early mechanical qualification). For an element PFM the embedded equipment should be acceptance tested (6.1e).

#### Retest after a nonconformance or after a design change following qualification

`ecss-e-st-10-03c-rev1.rule.retest_after_anomaly_or_design_change` · retest · §4.6.2, p. 39, pdf 39 · confidence: verified · [standards/ecss-e-st-10-03c-rev1/rules/retesting.yaml](standards/ecss-e-st-10-03c-rev1/rules/retesting.yaml)

Retesting is defined case by case because its scope varies (4.6.1); ECSS-E-ST-10-02 lists the situations that call for re-verification. After a failure or anomaly, all nonconformances are handled per ECSS-Q-ST-10-09 and the nonconformance review board decides whether and how far to retest to prove the disposition (4.3.4c). When a design modification is introduced after qualification is complete, the configuration control board (ECSS-M-ST-40) decides which part of the qualification test sequence is repeated (4.6.2a).

#### Retest after storage, before re-flight, and when qualification hardware is flown

`ecss-e-st-10-03c-rev1.rule.retest_after_storage_reflight_or_qm_flight_use` · retest · §4.6.3, p. 39, pdf 39 · confidence: verified · [standards/ecss-e-st-10-03c-rev1/rules/retesting.yaml](standards/ecss-e-st-10-03c-rev1/rules/retesting.yaml)

Storage after protoflight or acceptance testing: the supplier defines storage and post-storage test requirements (4.6.3a); periodic tests are scheduled for equipment degradation and personnel know-how (4.6.3b) and cover overall function, rotating parts, power consumption, TT&C through test caps, propulsion pressure via telemetry, visual inspection of separately stored equipment and contamination probes (4.6.3d); additional tests and the storage procedure need customer approval (4.6.3e-f); solar arrays should be stored in gaseous nitrogen (4.6.3g). Qualification hardware is flown only with customer agreement, any added testing must respect residual life, and after refurbishment or disassembly it undergoes an acceptance retest agreed with the customer (4.6.5).

### Tailoring provisions

#### 4.10.2 exemption - acceptance thermal vacuum folded into thermal cycling for vacuum-insensitive electronic units

`ssc-s-016.provision.tv_exemption_4_10_2` · Alternative Approach to Vacuum Testing Electronic Units (deck slide 41), §4.10.2, p. 41, pdf 42 · confidence: deck_only · [standards/ssc-s-016/tailoring/thermal-tailoring.yaml](standards/ssc-s-016/tailoring/thermal-tailoring.yaml)

Paragraph 4.10.2 lets acceptance electronic units waive the thermal vacuum test and combine its cycles into the less expensive thermal cycle test when the unit design has been demonstrated to be insensitive to vacuum: temperatures nearly identical in vacuum and in air, performance unaffected by vacuum, and no RF, high-voltage or arcing-susceptible parts. The demonstration comes from the earlier qualification or protoqualification thermal vacuum testing, which is why those units always receive thermal vacuum.

- **combine_tests**: Run the acceptance thermal vacuum cycles as additional thermal cycle cycles, so the total cycle count is kept in a single ambient-pressure test. Constraints: Only for units shown insensitive to vacuum in prior qualification or protoqualification thermal vacuum testing; total cycle count and burn-in hours are unchanged.
- **delete_test**: Waive the separate acceptance thermal vacuum test for the vacuum-insensitive electronic unit. Constraints: Not available to qualification or protoqualification units, which must still perform thermal vacuum to establish the insensitivity; a unit with the vacuum_sensitive flag (RF, high voltage, arcing risk, low-pressure heat transfer) does not qualify.
- Approval: Not stated in the deck; the exemption is a paragraph of the standard, verify who approves the insensitivity demonstration
- Recorded in: Demonstration of vacuum insensitivity from qualification or protoqualification test data

#### Narrower unit test temperature range compensated by more cycles (equivalent screening)

`ssc-s-016.provision.narrow_range_more_cycles` · Equivalent Thermal Stress Screening (deck slide 35), §6.3.8, p. 35, pdf 36 · confidence: deck_only · [standards/ssc-s-016/tailoring/thermal-tailoring.yaml](standards/ssc-s-016/tailoring/thermal-tailoring.yaml)

When a unit's thermal limits prevent testing over the default range (for example a payload unit that cannot reach -24 to +61 degC), the unit is tested to its constrained range and the number of thermal cycles is increased to give the same screening and fatigue exposure, using the standard's equivalence based on an exponent of 1.4 on the temperature range. The deck notes this is the usual occasion for tailoring, more common for payload units than bus units, and that testing only to allowable limits reduces unit test effectiveness, demonstrates no performance margin and complicates higher-level tests.

- **reduce_level**: Test over a temperature range narrower than the default ESS range when unit performance and operation are constrained to it. Constraints: Only when the unit cannot meet the default range; must be paired with the increased cycle count below.
- **increase_cycles**: Increase the number of thermal cycles so that cycles times the temperature range to the power 1.4 matches the default requirement. Constraints: Equivalence per the standard's exponent-1.4 relation; the deck's curve runs from the default 14 (acceptance) or 20 (protoqualification) cycles at full range up to about 50 cycles near a 40-50 degC range.
- Approval: Not stated in the deck
- Recorded in: Not stated in the deck

#### Two-tier unit thermal testing - full ESS range with performance tests at narrower limits

`ssc-s-016.provision.two_tier_testing` · Test Temperature Tailoring - Two-Tier Testing (deck slide 44), §6.3.8, p. 44, pdf 45 · confidence: deck_only · [standards/ssc-s-016/tailoring/thermal-tailoring.yaml](standards/ssc-s-016/tailoring/thermal-tailoring.yaml)

A unit with well known and proven performance limits may be exposed to the full SSC-S-016 ESS temperature range while its performance tests are run only at a narrower performance range inside it. The hardware is screened over the full range but performance margin is not demonstrated, mission flexibility for unplanned flight scenarios is restricted, and retest or margin reduction after design changes is more likely; the deck says it should be considered only when performance cannot be demonstrated over the default ranges.

- **other**: Run specification performance and functional tests at a narrower hot/cold performance range while exposing the unit (survival, hot and cold starts) to the full screening range. Constraints: Only for units with well known, proven performance limits and only when performance cannot be shown over the default range; the screening range itself is not reduced.

- Recorded in: Not stated in the deck

#### Credit for slice/board-level thermal cycling toward unit cycle requirements, up to half

`ssc-s-016.provision.slice_board_credit` · Slice/Board Level Test Consideration (deck slide 40), §6.3.8, p. 40, pdf 41 · confidence: deck_only · [standards/ssc-s-016/tailoring/thermal-tailoring.yaml](standards/ssc-s-016/tailoring/thermal-tailoring.yaml)

#### Reduced thermal dwell when data or analysis show the whole test article reaches temperature sooner

`ssc-s-016.provision.dwell_reduction` · Proposed Tailoring - Thermal Dwell Times (deck slide 39), §6.3.8, p. 39, pdf 40 · confidence: deck_only · [standards/ssc-s-016/tailoring/thermal-tailoring.yaml](standards/ssc-s-016/tailoring/thermal-tailoring.yaml)

The dwell requirement (1 hr thermal cycle; 2 hr first and last thermal vacuum cycle, 1 hr intermediate) exists so that internal locations, which lag the base-plate sensors, are at test temperature before functional testing starts. The briefing's position is to keep the requirement but allow a shorter dwell at unit or vehicle level when data or analysis demonstrate that significantly less time brings all areas of the test article to test temperature.

- **reduce_duration**: Shorten the dwell at a temperature plateau before functional testing. Constraints: Only with test data or thermal analysis showing all internal locations reach the test temperature in the shorter time; starting functional tests while parts are still transitioning is an under-test.

- Recorded in: Data or analysis demonstrating the shorter time to temperature

#### Fewer unit acceptance thermal cycles only with hardware-specific reliability data

`ssc-s-016.provision.fewer_acceptance_cycles` · Proposed Tailoring - Fewer Acceptance Thermal Cycles (deck slide 36), §6.3.8, p. 36, pdf 37 · confidence: deck_only · [standards/ssc-s-016/tailoring/thermal-tailoring.yaml](standards/ssc-s-016/tailoring/thermal-tailoring.yaml)

Requests to cut unit acceptance thermal cycling (eight cycles is often proposed) are answered by the data behind the baseline: 14 cycles over 85 degC give about 95 percent test effectiveness (2 sigma), 8 cycles about 85-88 percent, letting two to four more latent defects escape to integration and one to three to flight. The briefing's recommendation is to keep 14 cycles and reduce only where hardware-specific data show the needed reliability is reached with fewer; card or slice thermal preconditioning does not by itself satisfy the unit cycle requirement.

- **reduce_cycles**: Reduce the acceptance thermal cycle count below the 14-cycle baseline. Constraints: Only when hardware-specific data demonstrate the required reliability with fewer cycles; a generic eight-cycle proposal is not accepted on test-effectiveness grounds.
- Approval: Not stated in the deck
- Recorded in: Hardware-specific reliability data

### Rules

#### Acceptance, qualification, protoqualification and development test regimes

`ssc-s-016.rule.regime_definitions` · model_philosophy · Test Levels (deck slide 14), §4, p. 14, pdf 15 · confidence: deck_only · [standards/ssc-s-016/rules/regimes-and-purposes.yaml](standards/ssc-s-016/rules/regimes-and-purposes.yaml)

Acceptance testing demonstrates the acceptability of each deliverable item, with the emphasis on workmanship and flight-worthiness. Qualification testing demonstrates the design, the manufacturing process and the acceptance program, at conditions more severe than flight, on non-flight hardware and before acceptance testing. Protoqualification (also called protoqual or flight-proof) is reduced qualification testing of flight hardware to save the cost and schedule of a dedicated qualification article; its thermal environment lies between acceptance and qualification. Development tests are engineering tests for design information, typically before formal qualification and unit-level testing.

#### Which thermal tests each regime runs and at which level of assembly

`ssc-s-016.rule.thermal_tests_by_regime_and_level` · model_philosophy · Thermal Environmental Tests specified in SSC-S-016 (deck slide 15), §6.3, p. 15, pdf 16 · confidence: deck_only · [standards/ssc-s-016/rules/regimes-and-purposes.yaml](standards/ssc-s-016/rules/regimes-and-purposes.yaml)

Thermal cycle (hot/cold cycling in air or GN2 with rapid transitions, purpose ESS) is the primary unit test and optional at subsystem and vehicle level; burn-in (continuation of thermal cycling, purpose ESS) is a unit test; thermal vacuum (realistic flight simulation, purpose performance verification and survival/turn-on) is the primary test at unit (vacuum-sensitive units), subsystem and vehicle level; thermal balance (flight simulation in vacuum, purpose thermal control verification and model correlation) is run on the vehicle and some subsystems. Qualification hardware receives thermal cycle, thermal vacuum and thermal balance but no burn-in; protoqualification hardware receives all four; acceptance hardware receives thermal cycle, burn-in and thermal vacuum but no thermal balance.

#### Objectives of thermal testing (screening, performance, turn-on, survival, model correlation)

`ssc-s-016.rule.thermal_test_objectives` · general · Objectives of Thermal Testing (deck slides 12-13), §6.3.8, p. 12, pdf 13 · confidence: deck_only · [standards/ssc-s-016/rules/regimes-and-purposes.yaml](standards/ssc-s-016/rules/regimes-and-purposes.yaml)

Thermal tests serve five purposes: environmental stress screening, which forces latent defects into observable failures under stresses more severe than use so they can be repaired before flight; performance verification within specification in a flight-like environment; turn-on demonstration under severe, changing temperatures and gradients; survival demonstration at the most severe realistic temperatures, where the unit must turn on and show no degradation once back in its operating range; and thermal model correlation of test data against predictions.

#### Thermal cycle and thermal vacuum are not interchangeable (perceptiveness by failure mechanism)

`ssc-s-016.rule.tc_tv_perceptiveness` · general · Thermal Test Purposes (deck slide 16), §6.3.8, p. 16, pdf 17 · confidence: deck_only · [standards/ssc-s-016/rules/regimes-and-purposes.yaml](standards/ssc-s-016/rules/regimes-and-purposes.yaml)

#### Vehicle thermal vacuum profile, performance testing and thermal zones

`ssc-s-016.rule.vehicle_thermal_vacuum_performance_tests` · functional_test · Vehicle Thermal Vacuum (TV) Testing (deck slide 57), §8, p. 57, pdf 58 · confidence: deck_only · [standards/ssc-s-016/rules/regimes-and-purposes.yaml](standards/ssc-s-016/rules/regimes-and-purposes.yaml)

Vehicle thermal vacuum runs four cycles for acceptance and protoqualification vehicles with specification performance tests hot and cold on the first and last cycle and the vehicle powered and monitored (trending, anomaly detection) during intermediate cycles and transitions. The temperature range is worst-case predictions plus the regime margin, with no default screening range; the vehicle is divided into thermal zones and performance testing in a zone starts when its first unit reaches the goal temperature. The test demonstrates mission performance and is not intended to screen units. The deck notes that programs sometimes waive full performance testing on the first cycle and recommends against it because trending from pre-test ambient to first and last vacuum cycle is lost.

#### Thermal uncertainty margin of 11 degC on thermal model predictions

Thermal analysis predictions carry a thermal uncertainty margin of 11 degC on both the hot and cold side; the maximum and minimum predicted temperatures used as acceptance test extremes are the raw analytic predictions widened by this margin. The deck gives the basis as 30 years of flight-versus-model comparisons across many programs, consistent with ESA and JAXA data, showing that 11 degC keeps 95 percent of flight temperatures (2 sigma) within the acceptance range; it says Class A and B mission analyses should include it.

#### Burn-in of 200 thermal test hours for acceptance and protoqualification electronic units

`ssc-s-016.rule.burn_in_hours` · operating_hours · Other SSC-S-016 Thermal Test Requirements (deck slide 37), §6.3.8, p. 37, pdf 38 · confidence: deck_only · [standards/ssc-s-016/rules/unit-thermal-rules.yaml](standards/ssc-s-016/rules/unit-thermal-rules.yaml)

Acceptance and protoqualification electronic units accrue at least 200 hours of thermal test operation, counted across thermal cycling, thermal vacuum and elevated-temperature operation, with at least 100 hours on each redundant path. Hours short of the total are made up with extra thermal cycles (preferred for fatigue-driven defects) or dwell at the hot acceptance temperature (preferred for diffusion-driven defects). Operation at ambient temperature does not count because it gives little thermal stress. Qualification units have no separate burn-in requirement (enveloped).

#### Unit thermal tests follow the vibration or acoustic test

`ssc-s-016.rule.thermal_after_vibration` · sequence · Unit Acceptance Tests - SSC-S-016 (deck slide 6), §6.3, p. 21, pdf 22 · confidence: deck_only · [standards/ssc-s-016/rules/unit-thermal-rules.yaml](standards/ssc-s-016/rules/unit-thermal-rules.yaml)

Unit thermal cycling and thermal vacuum are performed after the vibration (or acoustic) test so that latent defects precipitated by vibration are found during the longer operating exposure of the thermal tests. The suggested unit acceptance sequence is inspection, wear-in, specification performance, leakage, shock, vibration or acoustic, leakage, thermal cycle, thermal vacuum, proof pressure, proof load, leakage, EMC, specification performance, inspection.

Sequence: after ['random_vibration', 'acoustic']; before ['thermal_cycle', 'thermal_vacuum']

#### Operation and functional testing during the unit thermal profile

`ssc-s-016.rule.unit_thermal_test_operation` · functional_test · Other SSC-S-016 Thermal Test Requirements (deck slide 37), §6.3.8, p. 37, pdf 38 · confidence: deck_only · [standards/ssc-s-016/rules/unit-thermal-rules.yaml](standards/ssc-s-016/rules/unit-thermal-rules.yaml)

The unit operates at the temperature plateaus and during transitions. At the plateaus it runs performance testing, hot and cold starts and turn-on demonstration; during transitions perceptive parameters are monitored for failures and intermittents. Full specification performance tests are run hot and cold on the first and last cycle and abbreviated functional tests hot and cold on intermediate cycles. Survival temperatures are demonstrated with turn-on at the survival extremes; performance need not meet specification there but must show no degradation once back in the operating range. A thermal dwell at each plateau ensures all areas of the unit are at test temperature before functional testing starts.

|---|---|
| specification_performance_cycles | First and last cycle, hot and cold |
| functional_test_cycles | Intermediate cycles, hot and cold |
| hot_and_cold_starts | Required at plateaus |
